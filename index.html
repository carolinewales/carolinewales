01

NFL Combine Performance Analytics

Excel · Statistics · Regression · Individual Analysis

A raw combine dataset doesn't arrive ready for hypothesis testing. It arrives with outliers, missing values, and skewed variables that will wreck a normal-distribution assumption if you don't check first. Working alongside two of my classmates, I profiled the data in Excel using formula-driven descriptive statistics, frequency tables, and visualizations across every variable. From there we moved through distribution fitting, skewness and kurtosis, and normal and empirical probability methods, then tested real hypotheses. A one-sample t-test found that combine athletes run 40-yard sprints significantly slower than the assumed 4.50-second benchmark (sample mean 4.515 sec, n = 1,168, p < 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of 4.510 to 4.521 seconds.

The final stage was a multiple regression predicting 40-yard sprint time from seven combine measurables, refined through backward elimination. Age and shuttle-run time both dropped out as statistically insignificant, which was the more interesting finding, since agility seems like it should predict sprint speed and didn't once the other variables were controlled for. The final model (height, weight, vertical jump, bench press reps, and broad jump) explained about 21.6% of the variance in sprint time (R² = 0.2165), with weight the strongest driver of a slower time and vertical/broad jump the strongest drivers of a faster one. It's the same discipline organizations outside sports are increasingly expected to apply to any operational metric they track: the dataset changes, but the rigor required to trust a finding doesn't.

02

AI Wordle Solver

Python · Machine Learning · Team Project

Two teammates and I built this Streamlit app for our Artificial Intelligence course in the spring. The app recommends the best next Wordle guess by combining two approaches rather than picking one: deterministic constraint filtering eliminates impossible answers against the known letter feedback, while a 100-tree Random Forest regression model scores the surviving candidates on six engineered features, including letter uniqueness and letter commonness. My main responsibilities on the project were working out the deterministic constraint filtering and training the Random Forest regression model. We deliberately layered the model on top of the rule-based logic rather than relying on it alone, so a player is never left without a usable suggestion even if the model fails to load. It's the same guardrail-plus-model pattern production machine learning systems lean on when a prediction absolutely has to degrade gracefully instead of breaking outright. In testing, the app lands on the correct word within four guesses most of the time.

03

eCommerce Product Feed Automation: Twin Lion Co.

Operations · Automation · On the Job

As a Digital Operations & Marketing intern at Twin Lion, I noticed an issue with our eCommerce sales right away: our product catalog lived in Lightspeed, but needed to reach Meta Commerce Manager, and nothing connected the two automatically. I built a Python script against the Lightspeed eCom C-Series API to extract, filter, and format product data for automated upload. I was entirely responsible for building this script, relying on online resources like codebases, Lightspeed documentation, and ChatGPT to figure out how to code every detail Twin Lion needed. The script runs daily and has measurably increased online orders while cutting a manual re-entry step out of the workflow entirely.

I maintain the script in my free time, and keep product listings accurate both on our main website and for export to Meta. This project is a small-scale version of a pressure retailers everywhere are facing: as sales channels multiply, systems that don't talk to each other stop being an inconvenience and start being a real cost.

Point of View

Every function is becoming a data function, and the advantage now belongs to whoever can turn information into a decision fastest, no matter the industry.

01

Judgment is the scarce skill now. Dashboards and AI copilots have made it easier than ever to generate an answer. They haven't made it easier to know whether that answer is right, or what to actually do with it. The people who matter most are still the ones who can ask the right question and validate a finding before anyone acts on it.

02

Data fluency isn't one department's job anymore. Every team, not just the analytics team, is expected to read a dashboard, question a metric, and make a case with evidence. That shift rewards people who can translate between technical rigor and plain business language, not just people who can build the model.

03

The most valuable skill is the one that travels. Methodology doesn't care what industry it's applied to. A hypothesis test, a regression, a clean data pipeline: the discipline is identical whether the dataset is sprint times, sales orders, or something else entirely. That portability is exactly what I'm building toward.
